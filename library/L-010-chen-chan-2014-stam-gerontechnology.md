# L-010 — Chen & Chan (2014). STAM Empirically Validated and Extended

## Why this is in the library

This is the **empirical workhorse** behind STAM. Where Renaud & van Biljon (L-009)
proposed STAM qualitatively, Chen & Chan (2014) tested and extended it
quantitatively with 1,012 community-dwelling older adults (≥55) and derived the
specific predictors that survive in a structural equation model.

It is the single most cited paper in older-adult technology acceptance and is
typically used together with L-009 as the canonical STAM citation pair.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Validated predictors of older-adult acceptance** (in approximate order of
   effect size in their SEM): facilitating conditions, attitudes toward life and
   satisfaction, self-efficacy, anxiety, health, cognitive ability, physical
   functioning, social relationships, and gerontechnology self-efficacy. Perceived
   usefulness and perceived ease of use (the classic TAM core) are present but
   **not** dominant.
2. **Anxiety is a measurable, modifiable construct** that suppresses adoption
   independently of ability.
3. **Self-efficacy mediates** between abilities and use — i.e. teaching the skill
   without building the confidence does not produce adoption.
4. **Attitudes toward life** (life satisfaction, sense of agency) predict adoption.
   Implication: framing matters at the level of meaning, not just utility.
5. The model is **largely culture-portable** — the Hong Kong Chinese sample yields
   broadly the same predictor structure subsequent studies find in Western samples.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite as the **primary empirical warrant** for the contract's emphasis on
  facilitating conditions, anxiety reduction, and self-efficacy building.
- Cite when defending the contract's rule that **scripts must name and address the
  emotional state** of the viewer (anxiety, hesitation, embarrassment) before
  introducing the task.
- Cite when defending the contract's rule that **a video must end with a
  confidence statement** — a concrete, viewer-attributable success.
- Cite when justifying that life-meaning framing ("here's why this matters to your
  day, your family, your independence") is not soft fluff but an empirically
  validated adoption predictor.
