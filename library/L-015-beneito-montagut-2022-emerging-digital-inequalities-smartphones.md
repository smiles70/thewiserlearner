# L-015 — Beneito-Montagut, Rosales & Fernández-Ardèvol (2022). Emerging Digital Inequalities

## Why this is in the library

This is the **most current empirical evidence** in the library on how older
adults actually use their smartphones — the dominant device through which the
channel's content and any onward AI tools will be encountered.

Drawing on cross-national fieldwork by the BCN Aging-Comm research program
(Open University of Catalonia / IN3), the paper documents the **diversity** of
smartphone use among older adults and the **second-level digital divide** —
inequalities that emerge **after** access is achieved, in *what* people do with
the device.

For the contract, this is the source that grounds the rule that the imagined
viewer cannot be a single archetype.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Heterogeneity is the rule, not the exception.** Smartphone use among older
   adults ranges from minimal (calls and messages only) to extensive (banking,
   navigation, social media, health). A single "older adult viewer" persona is
   empirically wrong.
2. **The second-level digital divide is real.** Access alone does not produce
   benefit; the gap is now in *use repertoire* and *use confidence*, not in
   ownership.
3. **Trajectories matter.** What a person does today on their phone is heavily
   shaped by how they were first introduced to it and by their immediate social
   network's use, not by age per se.
4. **The smartphone is the dominant computing context** for older adults in
   many regions — desktop and tablet assumptions are increasingly misplaced.
5. Inequalities are partly **structural** (cost, plan, region, language) and
   partly **dispositional** (confidence, perceived relevance) — both are
   addressable.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite as the **primary warrant** for the contract's rule that videos are
  authored to be watched on a phone, not just a TV or computer.
- Cite when defending the rule that **the imagined viewer is heterogeneous**
  in skill repertoire, not in age.
- Cite when defending the rule that **every episode names where it falls on the
  introductory–advanced gradient** so viewers can self-select.
- Cite when defending content choices that prioritise **repertoire expansion**
  (one new useful thing) over **technology novelty**.
