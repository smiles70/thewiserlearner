# L-002 — Baltes (1997). Selection, Optimization, and Compensation (SOC)

## Why this is in the library

This is the canonical, single-author formulation of **Selective Optimization with
Compensation (SOC)** — the lifespan-development theory that explains how human
development at every age proceeds through three simultaneous processes:

- **Selection** — choosing which goals and domains to invest in.
- **Optimization** — pursuing those chosen goals with concentrated effort.
- **Compensation** — using alternative means when prior means become unavailable.

SOC is the most widely cited theoretical framework for **successful ageing** in the
psychological literature and is foundational for the Geragogy Contract's stance on
**adaptive, not deficit-framed, learning design** for older adults.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. Development is **not** a story of pure decline in later life; it is a story of
   continuous resource reallocation.
2. Effective design for older adults reduces the **number** of simultaneous goals
   (selection) and increases the **depth** of investment per goal (optimization).
3. When a previously available means is lost (e.g. fine motor precision, working memory
   span), substitution by alternative means (compensation) is the normative path,
   not a failure mode.
4. Older adults already practise SOC implicitly. Good geragogy designs **with** SOC,
   not against it.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when defending **one-thing-per-video** scope discipline.
- Cite when defending pacing that allows for compensation (slower, repeated cues).
- Cite when rejecting deficit-framed language ("for those who struggle with…")
  in favour of resource-reallocation framing ("here is one focused way to…").
- Cite when defending a curriculum sequence that builds depth before breadth.
