# L-005 — Czaja & Lee (2006). The Impact of Aging on Access to Technology

## Why this is in the library

A focused, much-cited synthesis from the same CREATE team that lays out the
**access** dimension of older-adult technology use: who has it, who does not, and
**why a design that ignores access becomes a design that ignores older adults**.

It is the canonical reference for the contract's stance that the channel must be
watchable on **modest hardware, slow connections, and without ancillary apps**.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. The "digital divide" by age is real, persistent, and **not explained by interest
   or intent**. Older adults overwhelmingly want to use technology when it serves a
   meaningful goal.
2. **Cost, complexity, and lack of training** are the dominant barriers — not lack of
   willingness.
3. **Universal-design principles** (clear language, generous targets, predictable
   layout, redundant cueing, error tolerance) are not "accommodations" — they are
   the floor for any design that hopes to be used by older adults.
4. The design conversation must be framed in terms of **fit between person, task,
   environment, and tool**, not in terms of user "limitations."

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when defending the rule that videos must work on **basic devices** (older
  smart TVs, basic tablets) and **without** requiring the viewer to install,
  sign in to, or buy anything beyond the published platforms.
- Cite when defending **plain-language captions** as default, not optional.
- Cite when defending the contract's prohibition against framing older adults as
  reluctant or "left behind." The evidence is that they are willing; the design is
  what's failing them.
- Cite when justifying scripts that name and dismantle a specific access barrier
  before introducing a new tool.
