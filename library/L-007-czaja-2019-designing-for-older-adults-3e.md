# L-007 — Czaja, Boot, Charness & Rogers (2019). Designing for Older Adults (3rd ed.)

## Why this is in the library

This is the **third edition** of the canonical human-factors handbook for designing
products, software, training, and instructional content for older adults. It
consolidates two decades of CREATE empirical work (L-004, L-005, L-006) into
actionable design principles.

The first edition (Fisk, Rogers, Charness, Czaja & Sharit, 2004) and the second
edition (2009) are the most cited handbooks in the field; this third edition
updates the evidence base through 2018 with new authorship led by Czaja, Boot,
Charness, and Rogers.

For a producer, this handbook is the **single highest-yield reference** in the
library — every chapter ends in design recommendations grounded in primary studies.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Heterogeneity is the rule.** Older adults differ from each other more than they
   differ from younger adults on most dimensions; the contract must reject monolithic
   "senior" framings.
2. **Sensory floor:** type, contrast, and audio cues should be designed for
   **moderate, not severe** age-related sensory change — but always above WCAG
   minimums (see batch 05). Specific guidance on font size, contrast ratio, and
   audio level is provided in the handbook's perception chapters.
3. **Cognitive floor:** the design should not assume working-memory capacity beyond
   3–4 elements held simultaneously without on-screen support; should not assume
   divided attention; should provide redundant visual + auditory cueing for any
   instruction.
4. **Motor floor:** target sizes, dwell times, and gesture complexity assumptions
   must be widened relative to younger-adult defaults.
5. **Training and instruction principles:** modelling > description; concrete >
   abstract; incremental > comprehensive; errorless > discovery; spaced > massed.
6. **Affect matters:** anxiety, perceived control, and perceived relevance gate
   whether the content is engaged with at all.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite as the **primary** authority for design specifications in the contract:
  font size, contrast, audio leveling, target/caption size, on-screen text
  duration.
- Cite when defending the **modelling-first** instructional pattern (show the
  full path from start to finish, then narrate the steps).
- Cite when defending the contract's **errorless-learning** rule (every action
  shown must succeed; failure modes are addressed in dedicated remediation
  episodes, not embedded as cliffhangers).
- Cite when defending the contract's **anxiety-reduction** language register.
