# L-008 — McLaughlin & Pak (2020). Designing Displays for Older Adults (2nd ed.)

## Why this is in the library

While L-007 (Czaja et al., 2019) covers the full human-factors design space, this
handbook is **specifically about visual displays** — the part of the design space
that matters most for a video channel. It synthesises perception, attention,
and screen-design research into recommendations for typography, layout, colour,
motion, icon design, and display-based instruction.

The first edition (Pak & McLaughlin, 2010) was the originally cited work in the
field; this 2020 second edition adds a decade of evidence on touch interfaces,
mobile displays, and motion/animation.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Typography:** age-related changes in acuity and contrast sensitivity require
   larger, higher-contrast type than younger-adult defaults. Recommended specifics
   (sans-serif, generous tracking, generous leading, contrast ratio well above
   WCAG AA minimum) are given in the typography chapter.
2. **Layout:** older adults benefit from **fewer simultaneous elements** on screen,
   **stable** spatial location of recurring elements, and **reduced visual
   clutter**. Animated transitions should be slow and brief.
3. **Colour:** age-related yellowing of the lens reduces blue/violet discrimination;
   colour coding should not depend on blue-yellow distinction alone.
4. **Motion:** rapid motion, parallax, and auto-advancing content increase cognitive
   load and reduce comprehension; static or slowly transitioning displays
   outperform.
5. **Icons:** icons are not language-free; they must be either highly conventional
   or paired with text. Novel icon vocabularies are a barrier.
6. **Captions and on-screen text:** dwell time should be calibrated to slower
   reading rates; auto-advancing captions tuned to younger reading speed are a
   contract-prohibited pattern.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite as the **primary** authority for **on-screen text specifications**:
  font, size, contrast, dwell time, line length, leading.
- Cite when defending the prohibition on rapid cuts, parallax, zooms, and
  auto-advancing carousels in b-roll.
- Cite when defending the rule that every icon used in graphics must be
  paired with a text label.
- Cite when defending the rule that captions are calibrated to slower reading
  speed (no industry-default auto-timing).
