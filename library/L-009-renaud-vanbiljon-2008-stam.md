# L-009 — Renaud & van Biljon (2008). The Senior Technology Acceptance Model (STAM)

## Why this is in the library

This paper introduces the **Senior Technology Acceptance Model (STAM)**, the first
acceptance model purpose-built for older adults rather than retrofitted from
generic TAM/UTAUT. It is the seminal reference for any acceptance-related claim in
the contract.

STAM's key contribution is its insistence that acceptance for older adults is
**not a single moment of intention-to-use** (as classic TAM frames it) but a
multi-stage process that begins long before exposure and continues well past first
use.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Acceptance is staged**, not single-moment. STAM identifies six stages:
   *objectification → integration → conversion → exploration → identification → acceptance/rejection*.
2. **User context dominates.** Demographics, health, social influence, and personal
   experience exert larger effects on adoption than perceived usefulness or
   perceived ease-of-use alone (the two TAM core constructs).
3. **Facilitating conditions are decisive.** Without the right physical environment,
   support, and onboarding, perceived usefulness does not translate to adoption.
4. **Rejection is rational.** When older adults decline technology, the cause is
   most often a real mismatch between the technology and the person's goals or
   conditions — not a deficit in the person.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when defending the contract's rule that **every video is part of a journey**,
  not a stand-alone artifact. STAM's staged model is the warrant.
- Cite when defending **onboarding-first** content design (early videos establish
  the "why" and the support context before introducing any specific tool).
- Cite when defending the contract's prohibition against framing non-adopters as
  laggards.
- Cite when justifying the channel's emphasis on **facilitating conditions** —
  what to do when something goes wrong, where to get help, what to ignore.
