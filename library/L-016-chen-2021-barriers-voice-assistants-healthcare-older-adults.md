# L-016 — Chen et al. (2021). Barriers and Design Opportunities for Voice Assistants

## Why this is in the library

A complementary, more recent, and more design-actionable study than L-013. The
authors identify the **specific failure modes** that prevent older adults from
getting value from voice assistants — and the design and instructional
opportunities that close those gaps.

For a producer building instructional content, this paper functions almost as a
checklist of what to teach explicitly in early episodes.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Discoverability is the dominant failure mode.** Older adults frequently don't
   know what they can ask, and the assistants provide no scaffolding to help them
   learn. Implication: the channel must function as the missing scaffolding.
2. **Wake-word and prosody mismatches** are real but secondary; instruction can
   address them in roughly one episode.
3. **Privacy concerns are pervasive and warrant explicit treatment**, not
   reassurance. (Reinforces L-014.)
4. **Single-shot interactions fail more often than multi-turn ones**; the
   assistant should be framed as a conversational partner, and viewers should be
   taught to clarify, correct, and follow up.
5. **Concrete, life-domain framing** ("ask it about your medication", "ask it for
   help drafting a note to your grandson") outperforms abstract feature
   demonstration.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when defending the rule that **early episodes are example-libraries**, not
  feature tours — what to ask, why, and what the assistant does well.
- Cite when defending the rule that **every episode includes at least one
  "and then…" follow-up turn** — modelling that the conversation does not end
  after one prompt.
- Cite when defending the rule that **privacy is treated as a first-class topic**,
  with a dedicated episode rather than a buried disclaimer.
- Cite when defending the rule that **the channel teaches the conversational
  pattern**, not just the tool: ask, evaluate the response, refine, follow up.
