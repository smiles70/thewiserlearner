# L-014 — Knowles & Hanson (2018). Older Adults' Deployment of "Distrust"

## Why this is in the library

This is the **definitive reframing of older-adult digital non-adoption as a
considered, agentic stance** rather than as fear, ignorance, or generational lag.
The authors interviewed older adults who chose **not** to use specific digital
technologies and found that their "distrust" was articulate, principled, and
often well-grounded:

> Older adults are not refusing because they fail to understand. They are
> refusing because they **do** understand — and have weighed the costs.

This paper, more than any other in the library, governs the channel's editorial
posture: **respect the viewer's reasons**, including their reasons not to adopt.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Distrust is rational and informed**, not a deficit. It is shaped by accurate
   perceptions of privacy, manipulation, opacity, and the asymmetry of risk
   between user and platform.
2. **Older adults exercise discrimination** about which technologies they
   adopt. They are not uniform adopters or uniform refusers.
3. **Honesty about limitations builds trust faster than reassurance** does.
   Telling a viewer something is genuinely risky earns more durable adoption
   than soft-pedalling the risk.
4. **The job of an educator is to inform the decision, not to drive the
   decision.** A viewer who says "no, this is not for me" after a clear
   explanation is a successful outcome, not a failure.
5. **Agency is the point.** Geragogy that strips agency, even with kind intent,
   has failed before it has begun.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite as **the primary warrant** for the contract's editorial posture: every
  episode treats the viewer as a capable adult deciding for themselves.
- Cite when defending the contract's rule that **no episode ends with a sell**.
  The viewer's decision is theirs to make.
- Cite when defending the rule that **risks and limitations are stated plainly**
  in every episode that introduces a new tool — privacy, hallucination, data
  use, cost.
- Cite when defending the rule against using **urgency, scarcity, fear of being
  left behind**, or any persuasion device that bypasses considered judgement.
- Cite when defending the rule that **"no" is a valid outcome** — the channel
  succeeds when a viewer makes any informed decision, not only when they adopt.
