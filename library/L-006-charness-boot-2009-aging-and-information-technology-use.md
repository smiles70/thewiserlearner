# L-006 — Charness & Boot (2009). Aging and Information Technology Use

## Why this is in the library

A short, dense, often-cited *Current Directions* review by two of the most cited
researchers on aging and technology. It is the cleanest one-page-summary citation
to anchor the contract's most important repeated claim:

> **The bottleneck is design, not the older adult.**

It directly synthesises CREATE-era empirical work (see L-004) into design and
training implications a producer can act on.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. **Use rates lag, but interest does not.** Older adults adopt technology when the
   value proposition is clear and the on-ramp is supported.
2. **Training matters disproportionately.** Older adults benefit more, in absolute
   terms, from well-structured training than younger adults do — because the design
   gap is doing more harm to them than to younger users.
3. **Self-paced, repetitive, error-tolerant practice** outperforms speed- or
   discovery-based instruction.
4. **Confidence (self-efficacy) is the moderator** of whether learning transfers to
   independent use. Building confidence is therefore not separate from teaching the
   skill — it *is* the teaching.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when defending **explicit, repeated demonstration** in scripts (one screen
  action shown twice — once narrated slowly, once silently from start to finish).
- Cite when defending the contract's pacing rule (≤120 wpm) and the
  no-time-pressure rule.
- Cite when defending the **"named win"** rule (every video must end with a
  concrete thing the viewer has done or can do, named explicitly).
- Cite when defending the contract's prohibition against discovery-style "look
  around and find it" instruction.
