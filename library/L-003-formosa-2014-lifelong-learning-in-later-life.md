# L-003 — Formosa (2014). Lifelong Learning in Later Life

## Why this is in the library

Marvin Formosa (University of Malta) is among the most cited contemporary scholars
of **geragogy** and older-adult education. This chapter, opening the
ESREA-affiliated edited volume *Learning across Generations in Europe*, sets out
the field-defining position of later-life learning as a distinct domain that
requires its own pedagogical framework — not merely a downstream application of
andragogy.

The chapter is foundational because it (a) names and bounds the field of geragogy
in modern terms, (b) places it within European lifelong-learning policy, and
(c) explicitly distinguishes later-life learning from earlier-life adult learning
along motivational, social, and emancipatory lines.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. Later-life learning is **distinct in purpose**, not merely in pace, from adult
   learning generally: it foregrounds meaning-making, citizenship, and continued
   self-authorship over occupational utility.
2. Older adults are an **internally heterogeneous** population; "the elderly"
   as a uniform category is a contract-prohibited frame.
3. Geragogy carries an **emancipatory** dimension: education for older adults
   should resist ageist framing and assumed dependency.
4. Provision should be **needs-based and learner-led**, not extension of younger-adult
   curricula.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when asserting that older-adult learning is not a "slower version of adult
  learning" — it has different goals.
- Cite when defending the rule against ageist or deficit framing.
- Cite when defending heterogeneity in the imagined viewer (no monolithic "senior").
- Cite when grounding the Naani principle in published geragogy theory.
