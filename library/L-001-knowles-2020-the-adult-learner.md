# L-001 — Knowles, Holton III, Swanson & Robinson (2020). *The Adult Learner* (9th ed.)

## Why this is in the library

Knowles is the foundational source for **andragogy** — the proposition that adult learning
differs structurally from childhood learning. Geragogy is the field that asks the next
question: does later-life learning differ structurally from adult learning generally?
Establishing the andragogical baseline is prerequisite for any contract claim about
how geragogy diverges from it.

The 9th edition (2020) is the most current authoritative statement and adds chapters
on diversity, inclusion, online learning, and the adult learner in digital contexts —
directly relevant to *The Wiser Learner*.

## Core claims used by the contract

1. Adults are self-directed learners; they need to know **why** before they learn **what**.
2. Adults bring extensive prior experience that becomes a resource for, and sometimes a
   barrier to, new learning.
3. Adult readiness to learn is tied to real-life tasks and roles.
4. Adult orientation to learning is problem-centred, not subject-centred.
5. Adult motivation is predominantly internal.

These six andragogical assumptions form the rebuttable baseline against which the
Geragogy Contract will state where later-life learning diverges.

## How the contract should use this source

- Cite when asserting that adult learners (older adults included) require the **why**
  before the **how**.
- Cite when defending problem-centred (not topic-centred) lesson structure.
- Cite when defending internal-motivation language over external-pressure language.
- Do **not** cite as authority on age-specific cognitive or sensory accommodations;
  later library entries cover that territory.
